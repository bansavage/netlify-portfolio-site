Art Direction

The venue gives us jungle, candlelight and paper. The guests give us color.

02 — Typography

No script. Ever.

Three faces, fixed roles. Any printed piece the resort produces — menus, table numbers, signage, seating chart — uses these and nothing else.

No wedding script, no calligraphy face, no flourished italic. If a proof comes back with one, it gets rejected.

03 — Dress code

The part that does the work

Sixty people in black and beige on a resort deck looks like a conference. The same sixty in lime, marigold and magenta under candlelight looks like nothing else. This is the cheapest design decision available and the highest impact one.

A vague dress code produces black. So the invitation has to name the colors and name what to avoid.

Ask for

  • Saturated color — the palette above
  • Loose 70s tailoring, wide lapels, pleated trousers
  • Linen and silk with a sheen
  • Camp collars, bold ties, tinted sunglasses
  • Fluid draped shapes, kimono sleeves, deep V
  • Vintage jewellery, statement earrings, beaded bags

Steer away from

  • Black, navy, charcoal, beige
  • Tuxedos and black tie
  • Matching parties — nobody coordinates
  • Pastels and blush
  • Anything stiff or structured
  • White, obviously

Wording for the invitation

Dress
Dress like the place: loud, warm, a little bit 1974.

Think lime, marigold, terracotta, magenta, cobalt. Linen suits and long fluid dresses. Please leave the black tie and the beige at home — we would rather you were overdressed in color than correct in navy.

Send the palette image with the save-the-date, not the invitation. People buy outfits early.

04 — Papel picado

Same six colors overhead

Strung over the reception in the palette above rather than the default red-white-green. It moves in the wind, it photographs against sky, and it is the one decoration that adds to the setting instead of competing with it.

Ask the resort whether they stock it and who hangs it. Bought locally it costs very little; the labour is the real line item.

05 — Everything else

What stays off the table

Tables: bare wood or plain linen. Candles in clusters — pillars and votives. No floral centrepieces. Bud vases with loose greenery if anything at all.

Light: candlelight and warm string light only. No uplighting, no color wash, no monogram projection.

Flowers: her bouquet, and that is close to it. Ceremony backdrop stays minimal — the setting is the backdrop.

Print: menus and table numbers in the three faces above, printed on uncoated stock. Numbers, not names, for tables.
