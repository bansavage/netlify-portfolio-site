ENES
For the save-the-date

Dress like the place:
loud, warm, a little bit 1974.

The venue gives us jungle, candlelight and paper. You give us the color. Twelve references — steal the register, not the exact outfit.

01Saturated color. Lime, marigold, terracotta, magenta, cobalt, olive.
02Loose and fluid. Wide lapels, pleated trousers, draped silk.
03No black, navy, charcoal or beige. No black tie.
04Nobody coordinates with anybody.
Men · 01

The suit is the statement

Wide lapels, pleated trousers, nothing tight. Color instead of pattern — butter yellow, forest green, tobacco. This is the register: 1974 Italian, not 2020 slim-fit.

Both of these read expensive because of the fit and the color, not the label.

Men · 02

Collar open, sunglasses on

Camp collars and printed shirts do the work a tie usually does. Tortoiseshell and amber lenses. Nothing black, nothing corporate.

The floral shirt under a solid jacket is the single easiest version of this for guests who don't own a colored suit.

Men · 03

Older guests included

This is not a young-crowd-only look. Linen, a good tie, tinted glasses — it works at any age and reads as dressed up, not costume.

Useful reference to send to dads and uncles who default to a navy suit.

Women · 01

Saturated, fluid, one color

Long, loose, deep V, kimono or draped sleeve. One strong color head to toe rather than prints. Silk with a sheen catches candlelight.

These two are the clearest expression of the palette — lime and terracotta are both on our color card.

Women · 02

Color on the dance floor

How it actually looks in motion, at night, under warm light. Olive, magenta, feathers. Nobody matching anybody.
